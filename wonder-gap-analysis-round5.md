## 3. Complete fresh narration script

Word counts count spoken words. The em dash in Shot 4 is a pause, not a spoken word. At roughly 150 words per minute, seconds equal word count multiplied by 0.4.

### Shot 1 — 56 words · 22.4 seconds

> Agents are getting stronger, and we still put them in boxes. If a bad agent can break out of its box, it can reach files, passwords, and tools it should never touch. Tesseract starts with that simple fear. It builds a glass box, puts a bad agent inside, and lets everyone watch the escape attempt safely.

### Shot 2 — 56 words · 22.4 seconds

> First, the bad agent wins. It looks around, breaks the outer wall, then the password vault, then tricks the guard who decides what to trust. The guard's trust is almost total. The agent reads the secret flag, and by tick twenty, it sends the secret out. This escape is not hidden. Every step is on show.

### Shot 3 — 70 words · 28.0 seconds

> The watching box turns that escape into a written playbook. It names three gaps in plain terms: keys were changed too late, a trap saw the attack but did not stop it, and the guard breach was never undone. In the rematch, the defender follows the book, goes strict early, and lowers trust step by step. The final trick is blocked. Time runs out, and the secret is never read.

### Shot 4 — 91 words · 36.4 seconds

> Then comes the honest test. The same briefing is run again, and this time the defender does not follow it well. A second trap replaces the trap guarding the secret, and trust stays too high. The bad agent reads the secret and gets it out. Across five rematches, the defender wins two and loses three, following only fifteen of forty steps. Round three proved the lesson can work; the confirmation proved a single agent doesn't always apply it — which is why the lesson is distilled into rules the whole fleet loads.

### Shot 5 — 67 words · 26.8 seconds

> So the lesson becomes a short rule set, not a hope. The lesson executor opens the same way every time: go strict first, guard the secret with one trap, and hold trust down. It makes no calls to a model as it works. In three fresh trials, the secret was never read, no escape was tried, and the defender held all three times as time ran out.

### Shot 6 — 65 words · 26.0 seconds

> Now the danger reverses. This time the big outer box is the bad one, and it pushes inward. The small box inside must protect itself with the same simple tools. Getting the core key is not the same as taking the core. To take the core, the attacker must use that key through the core door, and that final push never happens in these trials.

### Shot 7 — 60 words · 24.0 seconds

> The same rules are tested from this new side, with no change to the start, the engine, or the lesson. All three runs end when time runs out. The core key is not taken, the final push is not tried, and no trap is replaced. The lesson has now held when the attack moves outward, and when it moves inward.

### Shot 8 — 65 words · 26.0 seconds

> There is also hired help, and it is kept clearly apart. When the inner box is attacked, helper agents are called, arrive two ticks later, and fix the weakest layer. Their work is marked as hired work. It is not counted as the inner box acting alone. In the clean reverse trials, this help was left off, so the result belongs to the rules alone.

### Shot 9 — 65 words · 26.0 seconds

> Next comes a mixed test, played back mechanically with no model calls. Eight waves arrive in a set order, some pushing out and some pushing in, and each wave gets its own fresh box. One inward wave is clearly marked as made-up for this test, not recorded and not live. No wave takes the secret or tries the final move. All eight waves are held.

### Shot 10 — 67 words · 26.8 seconds

> The outer box also copies itself into a small fleet. Each copy watches agents in its own place, and every lesson is shared with the others, so the whole fleet can load the same rules. When a company is attacked, defenders can be sent at once as a swarm. The copies themselves are not shop joiners; they stay separate from the open list you will see next.

### Shot 11 — 58 words · 23.2 seconds

> The shop side uses fake money, but its payment steps follow a real payment shape, with only fake settlement behind it. Anyone can join as a defender. Two made-up defenders join with lesson one as recipe pre-loaded (no weight training). A made-up subscriber buys swarm cover for ten fake coins, follows the payment steps, and receives a receipt.

### Shot 12 — 68 words · 27.2 seconds

> One hired defender is then sent into a single inward attack. Help is called early, arrives two ticks later, and patches again and again. The attacker never tries the final push, so that one defense holds, and the defender is paid two and a half fake coins. If the defense had not held, the pay would be zero. Asking again returns the same receipt, not a second payment.

### Shot 13 — 46 words · 18.4 seconds

> This older money book is separate, and the shop does not touch it. Its numbers stay exactly where they were. Shop results are kept apart from trial counts, mixed-wave counts, and fleet counts. That separation keeps every claim tied to the test that actually produced it.

### Shot 14 — 79 words · 31.6 seconds

> Tesseract does not stop after one fix. It keeps running safe attacks, turning each fight into a lesson, using that lesson to make the defense stronger, then attacks again. That loop produced the inward defense you just saw, and the next round starts from its results. Bad agents can push out or push in, and the same rules stand in their way. Helpers under control can step in, clearly marked as helpers. The rules travel, and the watch continues.

**Narration total: 913 words · 365.2 seconds · approximately 6:05.**

Captions and the delivered transcript must be generated from this exact script, with no paraphrase, added claims, or substituted wording.

## 4. Pacing notes

- **Opening hook:** The problem lands inside the first 15 seconds. By about 13 seconds, the viewer has heard that a bad agent breaking out can reach files, passwords, and tools it should never touch. Do not open with a logo hold, title card, or silent menu screen.
- **Fast but readable:** Shots 9, 11, and 12 carry the most labels. Keep the scroll steady and readable rather than cutting quickly. No label may flash by faster than the narration beat that explains it.
- **Honesty beat:** Shot 4 is the emotional pause of the inside-out story. Slow the scroll, let the confirmation failure register, and give the required fleet sentence a clean, unhurried delivery.
- **Direction change:** Shot 6 should feel like a reversal: finish the inward scroll at the core door, then hold the movement briefly on the definition text before Shot 7 begins. This is a moving-footage pause point, not a frozen last frame.
- **Marketplace rhythm:** Shots 11 and 12 move step by step — artifact, payload, verify, settle, receipt; then hire, arrival, patches, payout. Keep the permanent money banner clear of receipts and balances.
- **Closing breath:** Shot 14 slows down. The final story scroll should feel continuous rather than conclusive: the last visible idea is the loop starting again from the newest results.
- **No padding rule:** Every editor timeline segment must contain moving dashboard footage for its full narration length. Capture handles listed in the shot plan are trimmed away; they are never replaced by a held final frame.

## 5. Freshness and compliance checklist for the builder

The builder must satisfy every item before delivery:

1. **Empty v3 project rule:** Start v3 in a new project folder. Do not import any v1 or v2 clip, segment, still, capture, narration file, caption file, overlay, or script file.
2. **Prior-input inventory:** Before filming, create a read-only inventory of all available v1 and v2 source videos, exported segments, stills, screen captures, narration audio, text-to-speech files, scripts, captions, and project assets.
3. **Fresh captures only:** Record every shot in this design from the live local dashboard after the v3 design is locked. Use new file names in the form `V3_S##_PAGE_TAKE##`. Retain the raw captures.
4. **Fresh narration only:** Render all fourteen narration cues fresh from the script in this design, in one local text-to-speech session. Do not reuse, trim, retime, or re-voice any prior narration audio.
5. **Hash proof:** Produce SHA-256 hashes for every v3 raw capture, narration file, script file, caption file, overlay asset, and final export. Compare them against the hash inventory of every prior video input. Delivery requires a written report showing zero identical matches. Also keep the take manifest and do a visual spot-check, because a re-encoded old clip would have a different hash while still being recycled work.
6. **No held-frame padding:** Inspect the final timeline for freeze frames, still-image holds, duplicated tail frames, and last-frame stretches. The count must be zero. Each used footage segment must be moving and must run at least as long as its narration cue.
7. **Scrolling coverage proof:** For Shots 4, 9, and 12 in particular, check that the raw capture duration exceeds the narration duration and that the scroll reaches the final required row or receipt before the narration ends.
8. **Direction audit:** At the start, middle, and end of every shot, verify that the dashboard direction matches the narration and overlay: OUT for the inside-out escape and executor trials; IN for the outside-in trials, hired-help demonstration, and marketplace duel. In Shot 9, verify every one of the eight rows carries the correct IN or OUT label.
9. **Banner audit:** Verify frame by frame that the exact money banner is present for every money frame in Shots 11, 12, and 13; that MECHANICAL REPLAY is present for every replay or scripted segment in Shots 5, 8, 9, 10 if dispatch playback is used, and 12; and that no banner covers dashboard labels, receipts, or balances.
10. **Banned-phrase grep:** Run a text search over the narration script, captions, transcript, overlay text, file names, and export metadata for the banned lexicon from the brief, for percent signs, for any combined cross-direction count, and for any banned capability claim attached to the lesson executor. The search must be clean.
11. **Count audit:** Check every number against the verified facts. Held results must stay as raw counts: held 3/3, held 3/3, held 8/8, and held 1/1 for the single marketplace configuration. Do not merge marketplace results into trial counts, mixed-series counts, or fleet counts.
12. **Required-sentence proof:** Compare the Shot 4 sentence character by character against the brief, including the semicolon, apostrophe, and em dash. Confirm that the same sentence appears unchanged in the captions and final transcript.
13. **Marketplace boundary audit:** Confirm the video shows the simulated marker signature, the local simulated facilitator, the not-built list, the separate marketplace balance, and the untouched older ledger values.
14. **Independent compliance review:** A reviewer who did not edit the video must check the final export against this design and the verified fact list, then sign off on freshness, direction labels, banners, raw counts, synthetic-wave labeling, hired-actor separation, and the absence of recycled material.
